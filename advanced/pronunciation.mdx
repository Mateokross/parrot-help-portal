Even the best AI voices stumble on raw article text. `$4.5M` can get read as a string of characters, `Dr.` could become "dee are", and `3/4` is anyone's guess. Powerbeans pairs the most natural voices available with an optimization layer that rewrites text, so your articles sound like they were read by a person rather than parsed by a machine.

This happens in two stages: an automatic pass that works from your very first article, and a pronunciation dictionary you control.

## The Optimization Layer

Before narration, every article is adapted so that numbers, prices, dates, abbreviations, and symbols are read the way a person would say them. This is on by default, requires no configuration, and applies to every audio on every plan.

| Written | Read aloud |
| --- | --- |
| `Dr. Smith` | Doctor Smith |
| `$4.5M` | four point five million dollars |
| `on 3/4` (US) | on March fourth |
| `on 3/4` (UK) | on the third of April |

Date formats follow the conventions of the article's language and region, so the same digits are read correctly for a US and a UK audience. 

Adaptation is contextual, so `3/4` can be read as three fourths if it refers to fractions and not a date. 

This layer fixes the mispronunciations that affect most publishers, so common cases are handled for you without anyone having to report them.

## The Pronunciation Dictionary

Some words can only be settled by you — a columnist's surname, a local place name, a brand with an unusual reading, an acronym your newsroom says as a word rather than letter by letter. Each dictionary entry pairs the word as written with how it should be read, for a given language.

| Word | Read as | Language | Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Siobhan | Shivawn | English | Columnist |
| Leicester | Lester | English | Place |
| PSOE | pesoe | Spanish | Acronym read as a word |
| BBVA | be be uve a | Spanish | Acronym spelled out |

Some acronyms are read as words and some letter by letter, and which is which is a newsroom convention rather than something a voice can infer.

Entries apply to all audio generated for your site from that point on. Audio generated before an entry was added keeps its original narration.
